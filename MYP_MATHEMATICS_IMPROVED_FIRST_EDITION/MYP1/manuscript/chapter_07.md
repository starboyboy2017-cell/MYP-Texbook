Statement of inquiry Spatial relationships obey a small number of angle rules, and precise constructions let those rules be checked and reproduced in real structures.
Key and related concepts
Key concept · Relationships. Each angle fact relates two or more angles, which is why a single measurement can de-
termine many others.
Related · Space. Angles describe direction and turn, so the same facts appear in roofs, roads and folded paper.
Related · System. Triangles and polygons are systems: a few properties force all the rest.
Global context · Orientation in space and time
Exploration: how builders have established square corners and level lines without modern instruments, and why
the 3–4–5 triangle was used for thousands of years.
Inquiry questions
– Factual: What are the angle facts for a straight line, a point, parallel lines and a triangle?
– Conceptual: Why must the angles of every triangle add to 180°?
– Debatable: Is a construction with ruler and compass more convincing than a measurement with a pro-
tractor?

The first big result
The angles of any triangle add to 180°. Every other triangle fact in this
chapter follows from it, and it follows in turn from the fact that angles
on a straight line add to 180° together with the parallel-line facts.
7.1 Lines, angles and angle facts
Angle vocabulary
An acute angle is less than 90°; a right angle is exactly 90°; an obtuse
angle is between 90° and 180°; a straight angle is 180° and a reflex angle
is between 180° and 360°. Two angles that add to 90° are complementary;
two that add to 180° are supplementary.

7.2 Parallel lines and triangles
The angle sum of a triangle is not an independent fact: it follows from
the straight-line fact together with the parallel-line facts. Drawing a
line parallel to one side through the opposite vertex makes the proof

The reasoning, in three steps
1 Draw a line through the top vertex parallel to the base. 2 The two
angles between this line and the sides are alternate to the base angles, so
they equal them. 3 The three angles at the top vertex now sit on a
straight line, so they add to 180° — and those three angles are the three
angles of the triangle.

Angle sum of a polygon
An n-sided polygon can be divided into n − 2 triangles from one vertex,
so its interior angles add to (n − 2) × 180°. For a quadrilateral this gives
360°; for a pentagon, 540°.

7.4 Constructions with ruler and compass
A construction uses only a straight edge and a pair of compasses, with
no measurement. Its value is that every step is justified by a geometric
fact, so the result is exact rather than close.

Construction Steps Justification

Triangle from three sides Draw one side; arcs of the other two lengths from its ends; join the crossing.

Perpendicular at a point on a line
Equal marks either side of the point; equal arcs above; join.
The construction is symmetric about the point.
Losing accuracy through a blunt pencil
In a construction the compass point must sit exactly on the intersection, and the pencil must be sharp. A 1 mm slip
at the first arc can produce a visibly wrong bisector — and the geometry will be right even though the drawing is
not.

The triangle inequality
A triangle can be made from three lengths exactly when the sum of any two is greater than the third. If two sides
just reach the third, the triangle collapses into a straight line; if they fall short, they cannot meet.
ATL · Critical thinking
In order for a student to generalise from physical trials, students must record the boundary cases, because the rule
is revealed where a figure just succeeds or just fails.

What this chapter established
– Angles on a straight line add to 180°; angles at a point add to 360°.
– Parallel lines create equal corresponding and alternate angles, and supplementary co-interior angles.
– The angles of every triangle add to 180°, and this follows from the earlier facts.
– The interior angles of an n-sided polygon add to (n − 2) × 180°.
– Ruler-and-compass constructions are exact because each step is justified.
Vocabulary
Term Meaning
acute / obtuse / reflex Less than 90° / between 90° and 180° / between 180° and 360°.
complementary / supplementary Adding to 90° / adding to 180°.
transversal A line crossing two or more other lines.
alternate angles Equal angles on opposite sides of a transversal.
corresponding angles Equal angles in matching positions.
co-interior angles Angles between parallels on the same side, adding to 180°.
bisector A line dividing an angle or segment into two equal parts.
Misconceptions to watch
Three that persist
– Lines that look parallel are parallel. Only the marking justifies the facts.
– All triangles have a 60° angle. Only equilateral triangles do.
– A bigger triangle has a bigger angle sum. The sum is 180° at every size.
