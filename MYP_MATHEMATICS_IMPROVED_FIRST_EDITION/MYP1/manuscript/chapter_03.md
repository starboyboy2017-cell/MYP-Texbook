Statement of inquiry Whole numbers are built from factors in a structured way, and that structure makes it possible to divide quantities fairly, schedule repeating events and communicate results with exact precision.
Key and related concepts
Key concept · Relationships. Every number greater than 1 relates to the primes that build it, and that relationship
does not change however the number is written.
Related · System. Divisibility rules form a system: each one tests a specific structure in the digits.
Related · Pattern. Multiples repeat on a regular grid, which is why the lowest common multiple can be read from
the pattern.
Global context · Fairness and development
Exploration: how the structure of numbers decides what can be shared equally, from splitting a harvest among
households to scheduling two services that must not clash.
Inquiry questions
– Factual: What is a factor, what is a multiple, and how can you tell whether one number divides another?
– Conceptual: Why is prime factorisation called the “building block” representation of a number?
– Debatable: Is it fairer to divide a quantity using the highest common factor or the lowest common mul-
tiple?

3.1 Factors, multiples and factor pairs

Factor and multiple
A factor of a whole number divides it exactly, leaving no remainder. A
multiple is the result of multiplying that number by a whole number. So
6 is a factor of 24, and 24 is a multiple of 6. Factors and multiples are the
same relationship seen from two directions.
Factors come in pairs. Since 24 = 3 × 8, both 3 and 8 are factors, and
they belong together as a pair. The factor pairs of 24 are 1 × 24, 2 × 12,
3 × 8 and 4 × 6. The list stops when the two members of a pair meet or
cross, which is why you never need to test beyond the square root.
1 × 36 2 × 18 3 × 12 4 × 9 6 × 6
F I G U R E 2 The factor pairs of 36 as rectangular arrays. Every pair is the same 36 counters arranged differently.
Why the square root is the stopping point
If a × b = n and a < b, then a must be less than √n. So every factor pair has
one member below the square root. Searching up to √n finds every pair,
and searching further finds only repeats.
P R O B L E M Find all the factors of 48 and write them as pairs.
1 Start from 1: 1 × 48 = 48, so 1 and 48 are a pair.
2 2 × 24 = 48; 3 × 16 = 48; 4 × 12 = 48; 6 × 8 = 48.
3 5 and 7 do not divide 48. The next number to test is √48 ≈ 6.9, and we have already passed it.
4 So the factors are 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, 16, 24 and 48.
A N S W E R 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, 16, 24, 48 — ten factors.
Count them: ten. Pairs: 1×48, 2×24, 3×16, 4×12, 6×8 ✓

Why the digit-sum test works for 3 and 9
Any power of 10 is one more than a multiple of 9: 10 = 9 + 1, 100 = 99 + 1, 1 000 = 999 + 1. When a number is broken
into its columns, every column contributes a multiple of 9 plus the digit itself. So the remainders depend only on
the sum of the digits.
P R O B L E M Test 3 528 for divisibility by 3, 4, 6 and 9.
1 Digit sum 3 + 5 + 2 + 8 = 18, which is a multiple of 3, so 3 528 is divisible by 3.
2 18 is not a multiple of 9? It is: 18 = 2 × 9, so 3 528 is also divisible by 9.
3 Last two digits are 28, and 28 ÷ 4 = 7, so divisible by 4.
4 Divisible by 2 (it is even) and by 3, so divisible by 6.
A N S W E R Divisible by 3, 4, 6 and 9.
3 528 ÷ 9 = 392, and 392 is even ✓

3.3 Prime and composite numbers
Prime and composite
A prime number is a whole number greater than 1 with exactly two
factors: 1 and itself. A composite number has more than two factors. 1 is
neither prime nor composite, because it has only one factor.
Primes are the atoms of multiplication. Every composite number can
be broken into primes, and no composite number is itself a building
block. This is why primes appear in unexpected places: in the lengths
of repeating patterns, in the design of calendars, and in the encryp-
tion that protects a bank transfer.

Testing whether a number is prime
– Test divisibility by 2, 3, 5 and 7 first; most composites fail one of
these.
– If nothing divides it, test the remaining primes up to its square
root.
– Stop at the square root: if no factor has been found by then, none
exists.
– A prime can be large; a test does not need to try every number be-
low it.

Why primes matter beyond the classroom
Modern encryption relies on the fact that multiplying two large primes
is easy but recovering the primes from the product is not. A 200-digit
number built from two 100-digit primes cannot realistically be factor-
ised by testing, which is what protects the message.

3.4 Prime factorisation
Prime factorisation writes a number as a product of primes only. The
result is unique: 60 is 2 × 2 × 3 × 5 however you find it, and no other
product of primes gives 60. That uniqueness is called the fundamental
theorem of arithmetic, and it is the reason factor trees always agree.

Prime factorisation
The prime factorisation of a number is its expression as a product of prime factors, usually written with indices and
with the primes in order: 60 = 22 × 3 × 5.
P R O B L E M Write 84 as a product of prime factors.
1 Divide by the smallest prime that works: 84 ÷ 2 = 42.
2 42 ÷ 2 = 21. 21 is odd, so move to 3: 21 ÷ 3 = 7.
3 7 is prime, so stop.
4 Collect the divisors: 2 × 2 × 3 × 7 = 22 × 3 × 7.
22 × 3 × 7 = 4 × 21 = 84 ✓
P R O B L E M Use the prime factorisation of 360 to decide whether 360 is divisible by 24.
1 360 = 23 × 32 × 5.
2 24 = 23 × 3.
3 Every prime power in 24 appears in 360, with at least the same exponent.
4 So 360 ÷ 24 = 15 exactly.
A N S W E R Yes. 360 ÷ 24 = 15.
24 × 15 = 360 ✓

3.5 Highest common factor and lowest common multiple
Two numbers always share factors and share multiples. The largest
shared factor (HCF) answers “how can this be split into equal groups?”
The smallest shared multiple (LCM) answers “when do these cycles
meet again?” Both can be read directly from the prime factorisations.
Reading HCF and LCM from the factorisations
For HCF, take the primes common to both numbers and give each the
lower exponent. For LCM, take every prime that appears and give each
the higher exponent. With 12 = 22 × 3 and 18 = 2 × 32: HCF = 2 × 3 = 6 and
LCM = 22 × 32 = 36.

HCF(12, 18) = 6

P R O B L E M A harvest of 84 mangoes and 60 oranges is to be packed into identical bags with no fruit left over, each bag holding only one kind of fruit. What is the largest number of bags possible?
1 The number of bags must divide both 84 and 60, so find the HCF.
2 84 = 22 × 3 × 7 and 60 = 22 × 3 × 5.
3 Common primes with lower exponents: 22 × 3 = 12.
4 So 12 bags: 7 mangoes in each of 7 bags and 5 oranges in each of 5 bags.
12 × 7 = 84 and 12 × 5 = 60 ✓
P R O B L E M One water pump is checked every 12 days and another every 18 days. Both were checked today. In how many days will both be checked on the same day again?
1 Find the LCM of 12 and 18.
2 12 = 22 × 3 and 18 = 2 × 32.
3 LCM = 22 × 32 = 36.
4 So both are checked together again after 36 days.
36 ÷ 12 = 3 and 36 ÷ 18 = 2, both whole numbers ✓

The structure the table reveals
Multiples form a regular grid: row n is shaded every n columns. Primes are the rows with exactly two shaded cells.
The diagonal is shaded because every number is a factor of itself — which is precisely what makes the definition of
a prime depend on having exactly one other factor.

What this chapter established
– Factors and multiples describe the same relationship from opposite directions.
– Divisibility tests read the structure of the digits, and each can be explained.
– Primes are the multiplicative building blocks; 1 is neither prime nor composite.
– Prime factorisation is unique, so different routes give the same answer.
– HCF answers “equal groups”; LCM answers “when do cycles meet”.
Vocabulary
Term Meaning
factor A whole number that divides another exactly.
multiple The product of a number and a whole number.
prime A whole number greater than 1 with exactly two factors.
composite A whole number with more than two factors.
prime factorisation A number written as a product of primes.
HCF The largest number dividing two or more numbers exactly.
LCM The smallest number that is a multiple of two or more numbers.
Misconceptions to watch
Three that persist
1 is prime. It has only one factor, so it fails the definition.
The LCM is the product. Only when the numbers share no factor; LCM(4, 6) = 12, not 24.
A number ending in 3 is prime. 33, 63 and 93 all end in 3 and none is prime.
