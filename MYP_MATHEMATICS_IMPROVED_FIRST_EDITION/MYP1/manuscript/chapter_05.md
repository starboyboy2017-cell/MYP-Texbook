Statement of inquiry A percentage expresses a part in units of one hundred, which makes quantities with dif- ferent wholes comparable; that same act of standardising can clarify a decision or con- ceal what produced it.
Key and related concepts
Key concept · Relationships. A percentage relates a part to its whole, so the whole must always be named.
Related · Quantity. A percentage of a quantity is found by dividing by 100 and multiplying by the percentage.
Related · Equivalence. 25 %, 0.25 and 1/4 are three names for one number, and each makes a different calculation
easy.
Global context · Fairness and development
Exploration: how discounts, taxes, interest and aid targets are communicated, and how a percentage can be accur-
ate and still misleading when its base is hidden.
Inquiry questions
– Factual: How do you find a percentage of a quantity, and how do you express one quantity as a percent-
age of another?
– Conceptual: Why does a 20 % discount followed by a 20 % increase not restore the original price?
– Debatable: Is a “50 % off” label informative if the original price is unknown?

5.1 Per cent: a proportion of 100
Percentage
A percentage is a fraction with denominator 100. So 47 % = 47/100 = 0.47.
The symbol % is shorthand for “divided by 100”.

Forgetting that the base must be 100
A student writes 0.4 % for 0.4. Percentages are already hundredths, so
0.4 = 40 %. Reversing the direction of the conversion is the most com-
mon percentage error; check by asking whether the answer is bigger or
smaller than the original number.

5.2 Finding a percentage of a quantity
The method
To find p % of a quantity: convert the percentage to a decimal and mul-
tiply, or multiply by p and divide by 100. So 15 % of 240 = 0.15 × 240 = 36.
The decimal method is faster with a calculator; the fraction method is
often faster in the head. 25 % of 84 is a quarter of 84, which is 21 — no
calculator needed. Choosing the notation that fits the numbers is a
mathematical decision, not a shortcut.
10 % = 26 15 % = 26 + 13 = 39

5.3 Increasing and decreasing by a percentage
An increase of 20 % can be calculated in two ways: find 20 % and add
it, or multiply by 1.20 directly. The second method is shorter and,
more importantly, it generalises: a multiplier of 0.85 decreases by 15
% in one step.
The multiplier
An increase of p % multiplies by 1 + p/100. A decrease of p % multiplies
by 1 − p/100. So +20 % is × 1.20, −15 % is × 0.85, and +100 % is × 2.
P R O B L E M A bus fare of 2.40 rises by 15 %. Find the new fare.
1 The multiplier is 1 + 0.15 = 1.15.
2 2.40 × 1.15 = 2.76.
3 Check: the increase is 0.36, which is 15 % of 2.40.
2.40 + 0.36 = 2.76 ✓
P R O B L E M A quantity rises by 20 % and then falls by 20 %. Is it back to where it started?
1 Rising by 20 % multiplies by 1.20; falling by 20 % multiplies by 0.80.
2 Overall multiplier: 1.20 × 0.80 = 0.96.
3 So the quantity ends at 96 % of its original value: a 4 % decrease.
4 The two percentages applied to different bases, so they do not cancel.
A N S W E R No — it finishes 4 % below the starting value.
On 100: 120, then 96 ✓

The general result
Two successive discounts of p % and q % multiply: the combined multiplier is (1 − p/100)(1 − q/100). For 20 % and 20
% this is 0.8 × 0.8 = 0.64, a total discount of 36 %, always — the difference from 40 % is the discount on the discount.
ATL · Transfer
In order for a student to test a claim that “feels obvious”, students must compute at least three cases with different
starting values, because a single case can agree with a false rule by coincidence.

What this chapter established
– A percentage is a fraction over 100, so all three notations are interchangeable.
– A percentage always has a base, and the base must be named to interpret it.
– A percentage of a quantity is found by multiplying by a decimal or a fraction.
– Successive percentage changes multiply; they never add.
– Financial decisions require the calculation and a judgement about reasonableness.
Vocabulary
Term Meaning
percentage A fraction expressed in hundredths.
base The whole to which a percentage refers.
multiplier The single number by which a quantity is multiplied for a change.
discount A reduction from a marked price.
tax A percentage added to a price by an authority.
budget An allocation of a whole amount into named parts.
Misconceptions to watch
Three that persist
0.4 = 0.4 %. 0.4 is 40 %; percentages are already hundredths.
+20 % then −20 % is no change. The multipliers 1.2 and 0.8 give 0.96.
A bigger percentage means a bigger amount. 40 % of 70 (28) is less than 35 % of 80 (28 is equal, in fact) — always
compare the calculated amounts.
