Statement of inquiry A fraction, a decimal and a percentage are three notations for the same relationship between a part and a whole, and equivalence is the idea that lets us choose whichever notation makes the current question easiest.
Key and related concepts
Key concept · Relationships. A fraction records a relationship between two quantities, which is why the same rela-
tionship has many names.
Related · Equivalence. Multiplying numerator and denominator by the same number changes the writing without
changing the number.
Related · Quantity. A fraction of a quantity is found by dividing, then multiplying — in that order.
Global context · Identities and relationships
Exploration: how shared resources, recipes and measurements are divided, and why a fair share needs an exact
number rather than an impression.
Inquiry questions
– Factual: How do you convert between a fraction, a decimal and a percentage?
– Conceptual: Why does multiplying the numerator and denominator by the same number not change the
value?
– Debatable: Is it more honest to report a share as 1/3 or as 33 %?

The rule you found
Multiplying the numerator and the denominator by the same number
produces an equivalent fraction, because the number of parts and the
number taken both increase in the same proportion.
4.1 Equivalent fractions and simplest form
Equivalent fractions
Two fractions are equivalent when they name the same point on the
number line. Multiplying or dividing both the numerator and denomin-
ator by the same non-zero number produces an equivalent fraction: 3/4
= 6/8 = 9/12.
Simplest form (also called lowest terms) is the equivalent fraction in
which the numerator and denominator share no factor other than 1. It
is not the “best” fraction in every context — 6/8 is easier to add to 1/8 —
but it is the standard form for an answer.
1 Find the highest common factor of 36 and 48.
2 36 = 22 × 32 and 48 = 24 × 3, so the HCF is 22 × 3 = 12.
3 Divide both parts by 12: 36 ÷ 12 = 3 and 48 ÷ 12 = 4.
4 So 36/48 = 3/4.
3 × 12 = 36 and 4 × 12 = 48, so the fractions are equivalent ✓

4.3 Adding and subtracting fractions
You cannot add quantities measured in different units, and a fraction
is a measurement in units of 1/n. Adding 1/2 and 1/3 requires rewrit-
ing both in units of 1/6, exactly as adding 1 m and 30 cm requires con-
verting to the same unit first.
1/2 = 3/6
1/3 = 2/6 5/6
sum: 3/6 + 2/6 = 5/6 F I G U R E 3 1/2 + 1/3 becomes 3/6 + 2/6. The common denominator is the common unit.
The method
To add or subtract fractions: 1 find a common denominator (the LCM of
the denominators is efficient but any common multiple works); 2 re-
write each fraction with that denominator; 3 add or subtract the numer-
ators; 4 simplify if possible.

4.4 Multiplying and dividing fractions
Multiplication of fractions needs no common denominator, because
multiplying does not combine unlike units — it takes a part of a part. A
third of a half is a sixth: 1/3 × 1/2 = 1/6, and the picture makes the reas-
on obvious.
1/3 of the shaded half = 1/6 3/4 × 1/3 = 3/12 = 1/4
F I G U R E 4 A third of a half is one sixth. Multiplying numerators and denominators corresponds to multiplying the numbers of rows and columns in the grid.
The rules
Multiply: multiply numerators together and denominators together.
Simplify before multiplying if you can. Divide: multiply by the reciproc-
al of the second fraction — 3/4 ÷ 2/5 = 3/4 × 5/2. The reciprocal of a frac-
tion is the fraction turned upside down.
Why division becomes multiplication
Dividing by 2/5 asks “how many two-fifths fit into the quantity?” One
whole contains 5/2 halves of two-fifths, so dividing by 2/5 is the same as
multiplying by 5/2. Division by a fraction counts how many of that frac-
tion fit.

4.5 Decimals: place value, ordering and calculation
A decimal is a fraction written in place value. 0.37 means 37/100, and
this single fact connects every decimal rule to something already
known about fractions. Adding decimals works column by column be-
cause adding fractions with the same denominator works numerator
by numerator.
F I G U R E 5 A hundred square with 37 cells shaded: 0.37 = 37/100. The grid makes the connection between place value and fraction visible.
Calculating with decimals
– Adding and subtracting: align the decimal points, fill empty
columns with zeros, then calculate as for whole numbers.
– Multiplying: multiply as whole numbers, then count the total num-
ber of decimal places in both factors and place the point accord-
ingly. 0.3 × 0.4 = 0.12, not 1.2.
– Dividing: make the divisor a whole number by multiplying both
numbers by the same power of ten, then divide.
1 Ignore the points: 24 × 35 = 840.
2 Count decimal places: 1 + 2 = 3.
3 Place the point three places from the right: 0.840 = 0.84.
Estimate: 2.5 × 0.35 ≈ 0.875, and 0.84 is close ✓

Converting
Fraction to decimal: divide the numerator by the denominator. Decimal
to fraction: write over the appropriate power of ten and simplify — 0.36 =
36/100 = 9/25. Fraction to percentage: convert to a decimal and multiply
by 100.
Because the conversion is division, every fraction must do one of two
things: terminate, or repeat. Which happens depends not on the nu-
merator but on the denominator once the fraction is in simplest form.
Which fractions terminate?
A fraction in simplest form gives a terminating decimal exactly when its
denominator has no prime factors other than 2 and 5. So 3/8 terminates
(8 = 23) and 7/20 terminates (20 = 22 × 5), but 1/3 and 5/6 recur, because 3
and 6 contain the prime 3.
P R O B L E M Write (a) 5/8 and (b) 2/3 as decimals, and state whether each ter- minates.
1 (a) 5 ÷ 8 = 0.625; 8 = 23, so the decimal terminates.
2 (b) 2 ÷ 3 = 0.666…; the remainder 2 repeats at every step, so the decimal recurs: 0.6 with a dot over the 6.
3 A recurring decimal is usually written to three decimal places with a note, or with a dot or bar to show the repeating block.
A N S W E R (a) 0.625, terminating (b) 0.666…, recurring
0.625 × 8 = 5 ✓
P R O B L E M Write 0.45 as a fraction in simplest form.
1 0.45 = 45/100.
2 HCF of 45 and 100 is 5.
3 45 ÷ 5 = 9 and 100 ÷ 5 = 20.
4 So 0.45 = 9/20.
9 ÷ 20 = 0.45 ✓

The rule the investigation produces
A fraction in simplest form terminates exactly when the denominator is built from 2s and 5s only. Any other prime
in the denominator forces a repeating block, because the remainders must eventually cycle.

What this chapter established
– Equivalence comes from multiplying or dividing both parts by the same number.
– Comparing fractions needs a common unit, a landmark or a decimal.
– Adding and subtracting require a common denominator; multiplying and dividing do not.
– A decimal is a fraction in place-value notation, so decimal rules are fraction rules.
– A fraction terminates exactly when its denominator has no prime factors other than 2 and 5.
Vocabulary
Term Meaning
numerator The number of equal parts taken.
denominator The number of equal parts in the whole.
equivalent fractions Fractions naming the same value.
simplest form The equivalent fraction with no common factor.
reciprocal The fraction turned upside down; the number that multiplies to 1.
terminating decimal A decimal that ends.
recurring decimal A decimal with a repeating block of digits.
Misconceptions to watch
Three that persist
Add the tops and the bottoms. A fifth is not a common unit for halves and thirds.
Multiplying makes numbers bigger. Multiplying by a fraction less than 1 makes the result smaller.
0.5 × 0.5 = 2.5. Two decimal places in the factors give two places in the answer.
