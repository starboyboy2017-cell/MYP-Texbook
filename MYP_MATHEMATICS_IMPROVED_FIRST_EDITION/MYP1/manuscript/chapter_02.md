Two patterns, one structure
Powers of ten grow by adding one zero per step, so the exponent counts
the zeros. Powers of two double at each step, so the exponent counts the
doublings. In both cases the exponent counts how many copies of the
base are multiplied — that is the definition, and every pattern follows
from it.
2.1 From repeated multiplication to powers
A power is not a new kind of number. It is a shorter way of writing a multiplication in which every factor is the same.
Base, exponent, power
In an, the base a is the number being multiplied and the exponent n is
the number of copies. So an = a × a × … × a, with n factors. The whole ex-
pression is called a power of a.
Read 34 as “three to the power four” or “three to the fourth”. It means 3
× 3 × 3 × 3 = 81. Read 34 carefully: it is not 3 × 4. The exponent counts
factors, not additions.
3^4 means 4 factors of 3
3 × 3 × 3 × 3 = 81
F I G U R E 2 34 as four factors of 3. The exponent counts the factors, not their total.
Multiplying the base by the exponent
34 = 81, not 12. A useful check: 32 = 9 and 33 = 27, so 34 must be larger
again. If your answer is only 12, you have multiplied instead of repeated
the multiplication.

The two special exponents
– Exponent 1 means one factor: a1 = a. There is nothing to repeat.
– Exponent 0 means no factors at all, and the value is 1 for every
non-zero base. This is not arbitrary: 103 ÷ 10 = 102, 102 ÷ 10 = 101, and
continuing the pattern forces 101 ÷ 10 = 100 = 1.
– Exponent 2 is read “squared” and exponent 3 is read “cubed”, for
reasons that become visible in Chapter 9.

2.2 Powers of ten and expanded form
Place value is a system built on powers of ten. Every column is ten
times the column on its right, so expanded form can be written with a
power in every term, which is exactly how large numbers are handled

2.3 Squares, cubes and roots
A square number is a number multiplied by itself. The name is not
decorative: 16 counters can be arranged in a 4 by 4 square, and no oth-
er square arrangement uses exactly 16. A cube number fills a cube in
the same way.
Square numbers count a flat square.
Cube numbers count a solid cube.
4^{2} = 16
a square of 16 counters 3^{3} = 27
a cube of 27 small cubes
F I G U R E 4 Square numbers are the counters in a square; cube numbers are the cubes in a cube. 52 = 25 and 33 = 27.
Square and cube roots
The square root of n, written √n, is the positive number that multiplies
by itself to give n. The cube root, written ³√n, is the number that multi-
plies by itself twice to give n. So √49 = 7 because 7 × 7 = 49, and ³√27 = 3
because 3 × 3 × 3 = 27.
Facts worth knowing by heart
Square numbers 1, 4, 9, 16, 25, 36, 49, 64, 81, 100, 121, 144
Cube numbers 1, 8, 27, 64, 125, 216, 343, 512, 729, 1 000
Why the square root is always positive
Both 7 × 7 and (−7) × (−7) equal 49, but the root sign √ is defined to give
the positive value only. In Year 1 you will meet √49 = 7; the negative
solution belongs to equation solving in later years.

2.4 Order of operations
Consider 2 + 3 × 4. Read left to right, it gives 20. Read with multiplica-
tion first, it gives 14. Both readings are possible in English; only one is
mathematics. The convention exists because an expression written
once has to mean one thing to everybody who reads it.
The agreed order
1 Brackets and other grouping symbols. 2 Indices (powers and roots). 3
Multiplication and division, from left to right. 4 Addition and subtrac-
tion, from left to right. An expression with brackets inside brackets is
worked from the innermost pair outward.
Multiplication and division have equal priority
24 ÷ 4 × 2 is 12, not 3, because division and multiplication rank equally
and are worked left to right. The rule is not “division before multiplica-
tion”; it is “whichever comes first, working left to right”.
1 Indices first: 32 = 9, so the expression becomes 5 + 2 × 9.
2 Multiplication next: 2 × 9 = 18, giving 5 + 18.
3 Addition last: 5 + 18 = 23.
Estimate: 32 is 9 and 2 × 9 = 18, so the total is about 23 ✓

2.5 Substitution and expressions with powers
A letter in an expression stands for a number. Substitution means re-
placing the letter with a value and evaluating the resulting expression
using the order of operations. The position of the letter inside a power
matters.
P R O B L E M Evaluate 3x2 and (3x)2 when x = 4, and explain why the results dif- fer.
1 In 3x2 the exponent applies only to x: 3x2 = 3 × (4)2 = 3 × 16 = 48.
2 In (3x)2 the brackets make 3x the base: (3 × 4)2 = 122 = 144.
3 The bracket changes the base of the power. Without it, only x is squared. The second result is three times the first because (3x)2 = 32 × x2 = 9x2.
A N S W E R 3x2 = 48 and (3x)2 = 144.
48 × 3 = 144 ✓

What the investigation shows
Doubling is a power of 2, and powers grow far faster than most people expect. After 20 folds the stack is 220 × 0.1
mm ≈ 105 m. The pattern is exponential: each step multiplies rather than adds, so the graph curves upward rather
than rising in a straight line.

What this chapter established
– A power records repeated multiplication; the exponent counts factors, not additions.
– Powers of ten generate place value, so expanded form can be written with exponents.
– Square and cube numbers correspond to square and cube arrangements; roots reverse them.
– One agreed order of operations makes a written expression mean exactly one thing.
– Substitution tests the structure of an expression: where a symbol sits decides what it does.
Vocabulary
Term Meaning
base The number that is multiplied repeatedly in a power.
exponent / index The number of copies of the base.
power A product of equal factors, or the whole expression an.
square number The product of a number with itself.
cube number The product of three equal factors.
square root The positive number whose square is the given number.
substitution Replacing a letter by a number and evaluating.
Misconceptions to watch
Three that persist
34 = 12. Powers are not multiplications of base by exponent.
Multiplication always before division. They rank equally; work left to right.
−144 = 12. Roots give positive values in Year 1; the negative solution arises from equations, not from the root sign.
