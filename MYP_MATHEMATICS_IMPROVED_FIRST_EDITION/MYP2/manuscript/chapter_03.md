## 3.1 What is a rate?

**Criterion A · I** · Related concepts: Relationships, Quantity

> A rate is a ratio whose two quantities are measured in different units. That difference is the whole point: a rate tells you how much of one thing happens for each unit of another.

### 05 · EXPLAIN

A *rate* compares two quantities measured in different units. A *unit rate* is that comparison reduced to one unit of the second quantity: litres per minute, kilometres per hour, grams per cubic centimetre, pounds per kilogram. A unit rate is found by dividing.

Because the units are different, they cannot be cancelled away as they are in a ratio: 240 litres in 4 minutes is 60 litres per minute, and the compound unit “litres per minute” is retained in the answer. Writing the unit is not decoration. It is the part of the answer that says what has been measured.

[Figure: flow_rate — A pump as a rate: capacity divided by rate gives the time to fill. The unit of the answer, minutes, follows from litres ÷ (litres per minute).]

Three rates that every other rate problem in this chapter is built on: **speed** = distance ÷ time; **density** = mass ÷ volume; **unit price** = price ÷ quantity. Each can be rearranged, and each keeps its compound unit in the answer.

**Worked example 3.1.1 — Find and interpret a unit rate**

A printer produces 480 pages in 6 minutes. Find the unit rate and the time needed for 1 200 pages.

1. Divide: 480 ÷ 6 = 80 pages per minute.
2. The unit rate is 80 pages per minute; the unit is part of the answer.
3. For 1 200 pages: 1 200 ÷ 80 = 15 minutes.
4. Check by multiplication: 80 × 15 = 1 200 pages.

*Answer:* 80 pages per minute; 15 minutes

*Check:* Multiply back to the original total, and the units cancel as they should.

**Worked example 3.1.2 — Choose the better buy**

Rice is sold in 2 kg bags for £3.60 and in 5 kg bags for £8.25. Which is better value per kilogram?

1. Unit price of the small bag: 3.60 ÷ 2 = £1.80 per kg.
2. Unit price of the large bag: 8.25 ÷ 5 = £1.65 per kg.
3. Compare like with like: £1.65 < £1.80, so the 5 kg bag is cheaper per kilogram.
4. State the saving: 15 pence per kilogram.

*Answer:* The 5 kg bag, at £1.65 per kg against £1.80 per kg

*Check:* Check by total: 5 kg at £1.80 would cost £9.00, and the bag costs £8.25.

**Worked example 3.1.3 — A rate with a mixed unit**

A cyclist covers 27 km in 1 hour 30 minutes. Find the average speed in km/h.

1. Convert the time to hours: 1 hour 30 minutes = 1.5 hours.
2. Speed = distance ÷ time = 27 ÷ 1.5 = 18.
3. The unit is kilometres per hour: 18 km/h.
4. Check: 18 × 1.5 = 27 km, the original distance.

*Answer:* 18 km/h

*Check:* Always convert the time unit before dividing, or the answer will carry the wrong unit.

“480 pages in 6 minutes is 480 ÷ 6 = 80, so 80 pages.” The number is right; the answer is not. A rate without its unit is uninterpretable. Write “80 pages per minute”, or use the compound unit pages/min.

## 3.2 Unit prices and best buys

**Criterion A · II · D · II** · Related concepts: Quantity, Equivalence

> Comparing prices is a rate problem, and the fair comparison is almost never the one the shelf label invites you to make.

[Figure: price_bars — Four pack sizes with their prices. The tallest bar is not the best value: the comparison must be made per unit of quantity, not per pack.]

### The comparison routine

1. Convert every price to the same unit of quantity — usually 100 g or 1 kg, or 100 ml or 1 litre.
2. Divide price by quantity for each product.
3. Compare the unit prices and state the winner with the units.
4. Check the decision makes sense: a bulk pack can be worse value if it will not be used.

**Worked example 3.2.1 — Compare three pack sizes**

Pasta is sold at £0.95 for 500 g, £1.70 for 1 kg and £2.40 for 1.5 kg. Which is the best value?

1. Convert all to price per 100 g: 0.95 ÷ 5 = £0.19; 1.70 ÷ 10 = £0.17; 2.40 ÷ 15 = £0.16.
2. The 1.5 kg pack has the lowest price per 100 g.
3. The saving against the 500 g pack is 3p per 100 g, or 30p per kilogram.
4. State the answer with the comparison unit: £0.16 per 100 g.

*Answer:* The 1.5 kg pack, at £0.16 per 100 g

*Check:* Cross-check: 1.5 kg at £0.19 per 100 g would cost £2.85, and the pack costs £2.40.

**Worked example 3.2.2 — The same rate in different units**

A car uses 6.4 litres per 100 km. Express this in kilometres per litre.

1. 100 km needs 6.4 litres, so 1 litre covers 100 ÷ 6.4 km.
2. 100 ÷ 6.4 = 15.625.
3. The car travels 15.625 km per litre, about 15.6 km/L.
4. Check by multiplication: 15.625 × 6.4 = 100 km.

*Answer:* 15.625 km per litre

*Check:* Reciprocal rates are still rates; the unit changes but the relationship does not.

**Worked example 3.2.3 — A rate over a mixed quantity**

A 1.5 litre bottle of lemonade costs £2.10 and a 330 ml can costs £0.85. Which is better value per litre?

1. Convert both quantities to litres: 330 ml = 0.33 L.
2. Bottle: 2.10 ÷ 1.5 = £1.40 per litre.
3. Can: 0.85 ÷ 0.33 = £2.575… per litre, about £2.58.
4. The bottle is far better value per litre, though it may not all be drunk.

*Answer:* The 1.5 litre bottle, at £1.40 per litre

*Check:* Check the can: 0.33 × 2.575 ≈ 0.85, as priced.

“The bigger pack is always better value.” Sometimes it is not: shops price small packs as loss leaders, and a bulk pack that is wasted is not cheap. Compare unit prices, then decide with the use in mind.

## 3.3 Speed, density and flow

**Criterion A · II · C · III** · Related concepts: Change, Quantity

> Speed, density and flow are the three rates that carry most of the science in Year 2. Each is a division, and each can be rearranged to answer the other two questions.

$$ *s* = *d* ÷ *t*          *d* = *s t*          *t* = *d* ÷ *s* $$
*speed = distance ÷ time, and its two rearrangements*

$$ ρ = *m* ÷ *V*          *m* = ρ*V*          *V* = *m* ÷ ρ $$
*density = mass ÷ volume, in g/cm³ or kg/m³*

[Figure: speed_graph — Distance against time at a steady 12 km/h. The gradient of the line is the speed, which is why the unit of a gradient is a rate.]

A worked example in this section always states the formula, substitutes the values with their units, and then gives the answer. Writing the substitution is what makes the working checkable — by you, and by a reader.

**Worked example 3.3.1 — Find the time**

A car travels 210 km at an average speed of 84 km/h. How long does the journey take?

1. Write the formula: *t* = *d* ÷ *s*.
2. Substitute: *t* = 210 ÷ 84 = 2.5.
3. The unit follows from kilometres ÷ kilometres per hour, which is hours.
4. The journey takes 2.5 hours, or 2 hours 30 minutes.

*Answer:* 2.5 hours (2 h 30 min)

*Check:* Check: 84 × 2.5 = 210 km, as given.

**Worked example 3.3.2 — Density**

A metal block has mass 540 g and volume 60 cm³. Find its density in g/cm³, and its mass if it were cut to 25 cm³.

1. Density = mass ÷ volume = 540 ÷ 60 = 9.
2. The density is 9 g/cm³.
3. For 25 cm³: *m* = ρ*V* = 9 × 25 = 225 g.
4. Check: the volume is just over 40 % of the original, and so is the mass.

*Answer:* 9 g/cm³; 225 g

*Check:* Density is a property of the material, so it is unchanged by cutting the block.

**Worked example 3.3.3 — Two rates together**

Tap A fills a tank in 6 minutes; tap B fills the same tank in 12 minutes. How long do they take together?

1. Convert each to a rate per minute: A fills 1/6 of the tank per minute, B fills 1/12.
2. Together they fill 1/6 + 1/12 = 3/12 = 1/4 of the tank per minute.
3. Time = 1 ÷ (1/4) = 4 minutes.
4. Check: in 4 minutes A fills 4/6 and B fills 4/12, and 4/6 + 4/12 = 1.

*Answer:* 4 minutes

*Check:* Never average the times. Rates add; times do not.

“One tap takes 6 minutes and the other 12, so together they take 9 minutes.” Averaging times assumes the taps take turns. They work at the same time, so the rates add: 1/4 of the tank per minute, giving 4 minutes.

## 3.5 Summary, revision and reflection

### What this chapter established

- A rate compares quantities with different units and keeps its compound unit in the answer.
- A unit rate reduces a comparison to one unit, which is what makes comparison fair.
- Unit price is a rate, and the largest pack is not automatically the best value.
- Speed = distance ÷ time; density = mass ÷ volume; each rearranges to give the other quantities.
- Rates add when two agents work together; times do not.
- A unit-price comparison ignores waste, quality and storage, and should be reported as a comparison of one thing only.

**Vocabulary to use precisely**

**rate** · **unit rate** · **unit price** · **speed** · **density** · **flow rate** · **compound unit** · **per** · **average speed** · **best buy**

### Misconceptions to close

| The mistake | What is really happening |
|---|---|
| “A rate is just a number.” | 60 means nothing until it is 60 km/h. The unit is the meaning. |
| “The bigger number is the faster rate.” | Totals are not rates. 240 in 4 minutes is 60 per minute, which is slower than 150 in 2.5 minutes (also 60). |
| “Together, two taps take the average of their times.” | Rates add, not times: 1/6 + 1/12 = 1/4 per minute gives 4 minutes, not 9. |
| “Comparing prices means comparing prices.” | Compare prices per unit of the same quantity, in the same unit. |
