## 1.1 Understanding ratio language

**Criterion A · I, II, III** · Related concepts: Representation, Ratio as comparison

> A ratio is not a pair of numbers that happen to sit beside each other. It is a statement about how quantities are related by multiplication, and the order of the statement is part of its meaning.

### 05 · EXPLAIN

A *ratio* compares two or more quantities multiplicatively. For quantities *a* and *b*, the ratio is written *a* : *b*, read “*a* to *b*”. The quantities must be measured in the same unit before the ratio is written or simplified.

There are three common ways to write a two-part ratio: in words, with a colon, or as a fraction. A ratio of 3 parts blue to 5 parts yellow can be written “3 to 5”, 3 : 5, or 3/5 when the fraction is understood as a comparison, not as a share of the whole. The notation you choose should make the meaning clearer, not merely shorter.

[Figure: ratio_tape — A tape diagram for the ratio 3 : 5. The whole bar has 3 + 5 = 8 equal parts. Blue is 3/8 of the whole, while the ratio of blue to yellow is 3 : 5.]

### Part to part, and part to whole

A **part-to-part** ratio compares two parts of a group. A **part-to-whole** ratio compares one part with the entire group. In the diagram above, the part-to-part ratio of blue to yellow is 3 : 5. The part-to-whole ratio of blue to all counters is 3 : 8. Confusing these two is the most common source of error in the whole chapter, because the second term answers a different question.

Order matters. The ratio of blue to yellow is not the same statement as the ratio of yellow to blue. Reversing the terms describes a different comparison, even though both ratios use the same two numbers.

**Worked example 1.1.1 — Reading the terms**

A paint mix uses 3 parts blue to 5 parts yellow. State (a) the ratio of blue to yellow, (b) the ratio of blue to the whole mix, (c) the fraction of the mix that is yellow.

1. The order of the words decides the order of the numbers: blue to yellow is 3 : 5.
2. The whole mix has 3 + 5 = 8 parts, so blue to the whole is 3 : 8.
3. Yellow is 5 of those 8 parts, so the fraction is 5/8.
4. State each answer with its unit of meaning: parts, not cups.

*Answer:* (a) 3 : 5   (b) 3 : 8   (c) 5/8

*Check:* Check the two parts add to the whole: 3 + 5 = 8 equal parts, and 3/8 + 5/8 = 1.

**Worked example 1.1.2 — Same unit first**

A shelf holds 45 cm of books and has 1.2 m of space in total. Write the ratio of the books to the total space in simplest form.

1. The quantities are in different units, so convert first: 1.2 m = 120 cm.
2. The ratio of books to total space is 45 : 120.
3. Divide both terms by 15: 45 ÷ 15 = 3 and 120 ÷ 15 = 8.
4. The ratio in simplest form is 3 : 8.

*Answer:* 3 : 8

*Check:* The books fill 3/8 of the shelf, which is a little under half — plausible for a shelf with space to spare.

**Worked example 1.1.3 — Which comparison is being asked for?**

In a box of 24 counters, 9 are red and the rest are green. Write (a) red to green, (b) green to red, and (c) red to all counters, each in simplest form.

1. Green counters: 24 − 9 = 15.
2. Red to green is 9 : 15, which divides by 3 to give 3 : 5.
3. Green to red is 15 : 9, which divides by 3 to give 5 : 3.
4. Red to all counters is 9 : 24, which divides by 3 to give 3 : 8.

*Answer:* (a) 3 : 5   (b) 5 : 3   (c) 3 : 8

*Check:* 3 + 5 = 8 parts, and 3/8 of 24 is 9 red counters, as stated.

Writing “the ratio of red to green is 3 : 5, so 3/5 of the counters are red”. The ratio 3 : 5 compares red with green only. Red is 3/8 of the whole box, because the whole is 3 + 5 = 8 parts. Always identify what the second term counts before writing a fraction.

## 1.2 Equivalent ratios and simplest form

**Criterion A · II** · Related concepts: Equivalence, Simplification

> Two ratios are equivalent when they describe the same multiplicative relationship. The numbers may change; the comparison does not.

Ratios *a* : *b* and *c* : *d* are *equivalent* if there is a non-zero number *k* such that *c* = *k a* and *d* = *k b*. A useful test is the cross-product condition *a d* = *b c*.

Multiplying or dividing both terms by the same non-zero number produces an equivalent ratio. This is the same operation that produces equivalent fractions, applied to each part of the comparison. A ratio is in **simplest form** when the terms are whole numbers with no common factor greater than 1.

To test whether *a* : *b* = *c* : *d*, check whether *a d* = *b c*. For example, 6 : 9 and 10 : 15 are equivalent because 6 × 15 = 90 and 9 × 10 = 90.

[Figure: double_number_line — A double number line for 3 : 5. Every mark on the upper line is paired with the mark directly below it. Multiplying both quantities by the same factor moves you to another equivalent pair.]

**Worked example 1.2.1 — Simplify and justify**

Write 42 : 56 in simplest form and justify your answer with a common factor.

1. Both terms are even, so divide by 2: 21 : 28.
2. Both are multiples of 7, so divide by 7: 3 : 4.
3. Equivalently, divide once by the highest common factor, 14: 42 ÷ 14 = 3 and 56 ÷ 14 = 4.
4. Check with the cross-product: 42 × 4 = 168 and 56 × 3 = 168.

*Answer:* 3 : 4

*Check:* 3 and 4 share no factor greater than 1, so the ratio is in simplest form.

**Worked example 1.2.2 — Find the missing term**

The ratios 4 : 7 and 12 : *x* are equivalent. Find *x*.

1. The first term has been multiplied by 3, because 4 × 3 = 12.
2. Multiply the second term by the same factor: 7 × 3 = 21.
3. Check with cross-products: 4 × 21 = 84 and 7 × 12 = 84.

*Answer:* *x* = 21

*Check:* Both cross-products agree, so the ratios are equivalent.

**Worked example 1.2.3 — Decide, do not guess**

Are 8 : 12 and 14 : 21 equivalent? Are 8 : 12 and 10 : 14 equivalent? Justify each answer.

1. Simplify 8 : 12 by dividing both terms by 4: 2 : 3.
2. Simplify 14 : 21 by dividing both terms by 7: 2 : 3. The ratios are equivalent.
3. Simplify 10 : 14 by dividing both terms by 2: 5 : 7, which is not 2 : 3. Check: 8 × 14 = 112 and 12 × 10 = 120, and these differ.
4. When the simplified forms match, the ratios are equivalent; when they do not, they are not.

*Answer:* 8 : 12 = 14 : 21, but 8 : 12 ≠ 10 : 14.

*Check:* Simplest form is a fingerprint: two equivalent ratios always share it.

“5 : 10 simplifies to 1 : 2 because 5 − 4 = 1 and 10 − 8 = 2.” Subtracting the same amount from both terms changes the ratio, because a ratio is a multiplicative comparison. Only multiplying or dividing both terms by the same non-zero number preserves it.

## 1.3 Ratio tables and double number lines

**Criterion A · II · C · II** · Related concepts: Representation, Scaling

> A ratio table organises equivalent pairs so that the scaling factor becomes visible. A double number line shows the same relationship spatially, and a graph turns the same relationship into a line.

### Building a ratio table

Each row of a ratio table contains one pair of equivalent quantities. To move from one entry to the next, multiply or divide both quantities by the same factor. A useful strategy is to scale through a convenient unit, such as one part, because that makes every other entry easier to find.

*A ratio table for lemon concentrate and water. The ratio 2 : 5 holds in every column.*

| Concentrate (cups) | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 | 10 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Water (cups) | 5 | 10 | 15 | 20 | 25 |

The ratio is 2 : 5 in every column. Multiplying both quantities by 2 gives 4 : 10, by 3 gives 6 : 15, by 4 gives 8 : 20. If a table is built by adding the same amount to each row instead, the entries no longer line up and the table no longer represents equivalent ratios.

### Using a unit rate

Dividing both terms so that the first is 1 gives the **unit rate**: the amount of the second quantity for each one of the first. For the concentrate above, 1 cup of concentrate needs 2.5 cups of water. Once the unit rate is known, any quantity can be found with one multiplication.

**Worked example 1.3.1 — Complete the table and scale**

A recipe uses 3 cups of rice for every 4 people. Complete a ratio table for 3, 6, 9 and 15 people, and state the unit rate.

1. Write the base pair: 3 cups : 4 people.
2. Scale by 2 for 8 people: 6 cups. Scale by 3 for 12 people: 9 cups.
3. For 15 people, scale by 3.75: 3 × 3.75 = 11.25 cups.
4. The unit rate is 3 ÷ 4 = 0.75 cups per person.

*Answer:* 6 cups for 8 people, 9 cups for 12 people, 11.25 cups for 15 people; unit rate 0.75 cups per person.

*Check:* Check with the unit rate: 15 × 0.75 = 11.25, as in the table.

**Worked example 1.3.2 — Read a double number line**

On a double number line, kilometres run 0, 15, 30, 45 and miles run 0, 9, 18, 27. How many kilometres are in 21 miles?

1. Each 9 miles matches 15 km, so the scale factor from miles to kilometres is 15 ÷ 9 = 5/3.
2. For 21 miles: 21 × 5/3 = 35 km.
3. Alternatively, use the unit rate: 1 mile is 5/3 km, so 21 miles is 21 × 5/3 = 35 km.

*Answer:* 21 miles = 35 km

*Check:* Sanity check: 21 miles is a little more than twice 9 miles, and 35 km is a little more than twice 15 km.

**Worked example 1.3.3 — From table to graph**

Paint costs £6 for 2 litres. Complete the pairs (2, 6), (3, 9), (5, 15), (8, 24) and describe the graph of cost against litres.

1. Every pair has the same quotient: 6 ÷ 2 = 9 ÷ 3 = 15 ÷ 5 = 24 ÷ 8 = 3.
2. The points lie on a straight line through the origin.
3. The gradient of that line is the cost per litre, £3.
4. A straight line through the origin is exactly what a constant ratio looks like on a graph.

*Answer:* A straight line through the origin with gradient 3.

*Check:* Check one point off the line: 4 litres should cost £12, and 4 × 3 = 12.

[Figure: prop_graph — Cost against volume for paint at £3 per litre. The constant quotient becomes the gradient of a straight line through the origin.]

## 1.4 Sharing and comparing with ratios

**Criterion A · III · C · III** · Related concepts: Quantity, Proportionality

> To share a quantity in a given ratio, find the value of one part first: divide the total by the number of parts, then multiply by each term.

### Sharing in a ratio

1. Add the terms of the ratio to find the total number of parts.
2. Divide the quantity by the number of parts to find the value of one part.
3. Multiply the value of one part by each term of the ratio.
4. Check that the shares add back to the original quantity.

[Photograph: ch01_tiles.jpg — A gridded facade. Every bay repeats the same proportions, so one bay measured gives the whole elevation without counting it.]

[Figure: share_bars — A quantity of 360 shared in the ratio 2 : 3 : 4. The bar is cut into nine equal parts of 40, giving shares of 80, 120 and 160.]

**Worked example 1.4.1 — Two-part sharing**

Share £72 in the ratio 5 : 3.

1. There are 5 + 3 = 8 parts.
2. One part is 72 ÷ 8 = £9.
3. The first share is 5 × 9 = £45; the second is 3 × 9 = £27.
4. Check: 45 + 27 = 72, and 45 : 27 simplifies to 5 : 3.

*Answer:* £45 and £27

*Check:* Recombine the shares and re-simplify the ratio: both checks pass.

**Worked example 1.4.2 — Three-part sharing from a difference**

Two amounts are in the ratio 3 : 8 and their difference is 45. Find both amounts.

1. The difference of the terms is 8 − 3 = 5 parts.
2. Those 5 parts are worth 45, so one part is 45 ÷ 5 = 9.
3. The amounts are 3 × 9 = 27 and 8 × 9 = 72.
4. Check: 72 − 27 = 45, as required.

*Answer:* 27 and 72

*Check:* The difference check confirms the ratio, not just the arithmetic.

**Worked example 1.4.3 — Comparing two ratios**

Which is greater, 4 : 5 or 7 : 9? Justify your answer.

1. Write each as a fraction of the first quantity: 4/5 = 0.8 and 7/9 ≈ 0.778.
2. Since 0.8 > 0.778, the ratio 4 : 5 represents a larger first quantity relative to the second.
3. Cross-product check: 4 × 9 = 36 and 5 × 7 = 35. Because 36 > 35, 4 : 5 > 7 : 9.
4. Alternatively write both with a common second term of 45: 4 : 5 = 36 : 45 and 7 : 9 = 35 : 45.

*Answer:* 4 : 5 is greater than 7 : 9

*Check:* Two methods agree, so the comparison is robust.

To share 72 in the ratio 5 : 3, dividing 72 ÷ 2 = 36 treats the ratio as two equal shares. The ratio tells you there are 5 + 3 = 8 parts, not two. Always find the number of parts first.

## 1.6 Summary, revision and reflection

### What this chapter established

- A ratio compares quantities multiplicatively, and the order of the terms carries meaning.
- Part-to-part and part-to-whole ratios answer different questions; the denominator of a fraction comes from the total number of parts.
- Quantities must be in the same unit before a ratio is written.
- Multiplying or dividing both terms by the same non-zero number gives an equivalent ratio; adding does not.
- A ratio table, a double number line and a straight-line graph are three representations of one relationship.
- Sharing in a ratio needs the value of one part, found by dividing the total by the number of parts.

**Vocabulary to use precisely**

**ratio** · **term** · **part-to-part** · **part-to-whole** · **equivalent ratios** · **simplest form** · **ratio table** · **double number line** · **unit rate** · **scale factor** · **share**

### Misconceptions to close

| The mistake | What is really happening |
|---|---|
| “3 : 5 means 3/5 of the whole.” | 3 : 5 compares two parts. The whole is 3 + 5 = 8 parts, so the fraction is 3/8. |
| “Adding the same amount to both terms keeps the ratio.” | It does not. 2 : 3 becomes 5 : 6 when 3 is added, and 5/6 ≠ 2/3. Only multiplying or dividing works. |
| “The larger numbers mean the larger ratio.” | 10 : 20 is smaller than 3 : 4, because 0.5 < 0.75. Compare the quotients or use cross-products. |
| “Sharing in a ratio means dividing by the number of terms.” | Divide by the number of parts, which is the sum of the terms. |

### Mixed practice · consolidate and extend

**Practice 1.M** · MIXED 1–12

1. Simplify: (a) 14 : 21  (b) 30 : 45  (c) 64 : 80.  [F]
2. Share 252 in the ratio 5 : 4.  [F]
3. A 3.5 m length of timber is cut in the ratio 2 : 5. Find both pieces in centimetres.  [P]
4. A recipe for 6 people uses 450 g of flour. How much flour is needed for 10 people?  [F]
5. Which is greater, 5 : 8 or 7 : 11? Justify.  [R]
6. A photograph 12 cm by 8 cm is enlarged so that its width becomes 30 cm. Find its new height.  [P]
7. Three friends share a prize in the ratio 2 : 3 : 6. The largest share is £180. Find the total prize.  [P]
8. A student simplifies 24 : 36 to 4 : 6 and says it is in simplest form. Identify the error and give the correct answer.  [R]
9. A juice blend uses orange, mango and water in the ratio 5 : 2 : 8. State the fraction of the blend that is water.  [F]
10. Two quantities are in the ratio 7 : 4 and their difference is 27. Find both quantities.  [P]
11. A map has a scale of 1 : 20 000. Two villages are 4.5 km apart. How far apart are they on the map, in centimetres?  [E]
12. The ratio of red to blue beads in a jar is 3 : 7. After 20 red beads are added, the ratio becomes 5 : 7. How many blue beads are there?  [E]
