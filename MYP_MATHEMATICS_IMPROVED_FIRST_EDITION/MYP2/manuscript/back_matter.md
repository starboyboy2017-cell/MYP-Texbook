Two groups can share a mean and differ completely. Comparing spread as well as
centre is what makes a comparison honest.
class A
class B
same scale, two distributions: compare the boxes and the whiskers
Two box plots compared. The median marks the centre; the box holds the middle half of the data and the
whiskers reach the extremes.
The lower quartile (LQ) is the median of the lower half of the data and the upper quartile (UQ) the
median of the upper half. The interquartile range (IQR) = UQ − LQ and measures the spread of the
middle half. A box plot shows the smallest value, LQ, median, UQ and largest value.
Find the median, lower quartile, upper quartile and interquartile range of 3, 5, 6, 8, 9, 11,
12, 14, 15.
1 There are 9 values, so the median is the 5th value: 9.
2 The lower half is 3, 5, 6, 8, whose median is (5 + 6) ÷ 2 = 5.5.
3 The upper half is 11, 12, 14, 15, whose median is (12 + 14) ÷ 2 = 13.
4 IQR = 13 − 5.5 = 7.5.
median 9, LQ 5.5, UQ 13, IQR 7.5
Do not include the median value itself in either half when the count is odd.

Class A has median 62 and IQR 8; class B has median 66 and IQR 22. Compare the two
classes.
1 Class B's median is higher, so its typical score is better.
2 Class A's IQR is much smaller, so its scores are tightly grouped.
3 Class B's scores are far more spread out: some are well above and some well below its median.
4 A fair statement: B is better on average, but less consistent.
B higher median; A far more consistent
Compare a measure of centre and a measure of spread; one alone is never enough.
The data are 12, 14, 15, 16, 18, 19, 21, 45. Find the IQR and state whether 45 is an outlier by
the 1.5 × IQR rule.
1 Median = (16 + 18) ÷ 2 = 17.
2 Lower half 12, 14, 15, 16 has LQ = 14.5; upper half 18, 19, 21, 45 has UQ = 20.
3 IQR = 20 − 14.5 = 5.5, so 1.5 × IQR = 8.25.
4 The upper boundary is 20 + 8.25 = 28.25, and 45 is above it, so 45 is an outlier.
IQR 5.5; 45 is an outlier
An outlier should be investigated, not deleted: it may be a mistake or the most important value.
“Both groups have the same mean, so they are the same.” 10, 10, 10, 10 and 0, 5, 15, 20 both have
mean 10, but one has no spread at all. Always report a measure of spread alongside the average.

Chapter 11 · Univariate data
What this chapter established
– Data are numerical (discrete or continuous) or categorical, and the choice determines the
display.
– Mean, median and mode each describe a different sense of ‘typical’; the range and IQR describe spread.
– An outlier moves the mean much more than the median, so the median is safer for skewed
data.
– The mean from a frequency table is Σfx ÷ Σf.
– Bar charts compare categories and histograms show continuous classes; axes must start at
zero.
– Comparing two groups needs a measure of centre and a measure of spread.
