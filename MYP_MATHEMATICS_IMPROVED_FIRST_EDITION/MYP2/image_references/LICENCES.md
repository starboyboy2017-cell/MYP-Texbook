# Licences and permissions

## Photographs

All 22 photographs in this edition come from free-licence collections. They fall into two
licence families, both of which permit commercial and non-commercial reuse, including in a
printed book, without a fee or a permission request:

**Pexels licence (Pexels.com).** Free to use, modify and distribute, including for commercial
purposes; attribution is not required but is given in every caption and recorded in
`IMAGE_REGISTER.md` as a matter of practice. The licence forbids (a) selling unmodified copies of
the photographs, (b) implying that identifiable people endorse this book, and (c) redistributing
the photographs as a competing stock library. None of these applies to their use here, where each
photograph is a small illustrative element inside a mathematical argument.

**Pixabay licence (Pixabay.com).** Equivalent terms: free for commercial use, modification
allowed, no attribution required, and the same three prohibitions on resale of unmodified copies,
endorsement, and redistribution as a stock library.

**Wikimedia Commons, public domain.** One photograph (a compass on a map) is a public-domain
work hosted on Wikimedia Commons; public-domain works carry no conditions of use at all.

**What is claimed and what is not.** This book claims no exclusive right in any photograph. The
copyright in each photograph remains with its photographer. The credit line printed beneath each
image ("Photograph: Pexels, free licence") is a courtesy attribution, not a licence condition.

**What was rejected.** Any candidate carrying a visible watermark, or coming from a collection
whose licence requires payment (Shutterstock, Vecteezy, Adobe Stock, Getty Images, iStock,
Dreamstime, Freepik, Magnific) was discarded and does not appear in the book, in the DOCX, or in
the final PDF.

## Diagrams and figures

Every non-photographic figure — 45 numbered figures drawn from 52 diagram functions — is original
vector artwork produced by the program in `build/diagrams2.py`. Each figure is drawn from
reportlab primitives (lines, arcs, polygons, text), so it:

- contains no third-party artwork or traced material;
- remains sharp at any output size, because it is geometry rather than a bitmap;
- can be regenerated exactly from source, and is regenerated on every build.

The diagrams are © SADIKOU MOUKODDIM, 2026, released for use within this coursebook.
