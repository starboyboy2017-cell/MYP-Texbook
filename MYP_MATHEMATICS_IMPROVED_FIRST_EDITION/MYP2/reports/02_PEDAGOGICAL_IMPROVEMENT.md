# Report 2 — Pedagogical improvement

**Deliverable:** *MYP Mathematics 2 — Student Coursebook, Improved First Edition*
**Comparison:** original First Edition (260 pp) against this edition (245 pp)

The purpose of this report is to say, concretely, what changed in the *teaching* and why each
change is an improvement rather than a preference.

## 1. A fixed instructional sequence in every teaching section

**Original.** Sections moved straight from a definition to worked examples. The exploration was
usually present but sat in the investigation pages at the end of the chapter, after the students
had already been told the method.

**This edition.** Every teaching section of every chapter now runs in the same order:

1. **Engage** — one concrete situation stated in two or three sentences, ending with a task the
   student can attempt *before* being taught anything.
2. **Explore** — a figure or table plus 5–7 numbered prompts that lead the student to notice the
   pattern.
3. **Explain** — the definition, the key idea, the vocabulary in the exact form to be used later.
4. **Worked examples** — three per section, numbered `x.y.z`, each with a stated check.
5. **Practice** — Support / Core / Extension.
6. **Investigate / Apply** — two investigation cards and one Criterion D card per chapter.
7. **Reflect** — summary, misconceptions, revision, self-assessment.

**Why it is an improvement.** The MYP criteria reward *inquiry* and *communication*, but a student
cannot inquire about something they have not yet met. Putting the exploration before the
explanation means the student has a reason to want the definition when it arrives, and the
sequence is identical across all 52 sections (41 teaching sections and 11 review sections), so the book becomes predictable to use.

## 2. Worked examples that model the reasoning, not just the answer

**Original.** A worked example typically gave the problem and then the answer, sometimes with two
intermediate results.

**This edition.** Every worked example (121 across the book) has four parts:

- **Problem** stated in words, with units;
- **Numbered steps** (3–4), each a complete sentence naming the mathematical reason;
- **Answer** in a boxed line, with units;
- **A check** in italics — a second route, a substitution, or an order-of-magnitude test.

For example, Worked example 7.2.1 subtracts the two known angles in one step and then checks the
sum is 180°; Worked example 8.3.2 finds a tank's volume in m³ and then converts to litres with the
conversion stated; Worked example 10.2.2 counts favourable outcomes from a sample-space grid
rather than guessing.

**Why it is an improvement.** The original's practice questions assumed a student could reproduce
a method they had only seen stated. A check line makes the method self-correcting, which is the
single biggest lever on independent work.

## 3. Misconceptions named, then closed

Almost every section ends with a **COMMON ERROR** panel that states the mistake in a student's own
voice and then explains what is really happening. There are 40 of them, one for nearly every
teaching section, for example:

- *"The nth term of 5, 8, 11, … is 3n, because the difference is 3."* — the difference gives the
  coefficient, not the whole rule.
- *"The area of a circle of diameter 10 cm is 3.14 × 10²."* — the formula needs the radius.
- *"Two coins give three outcomes, so P(two heads) = 1/3."* — HT and TH are different.
- *"Both groups have the same mean, so they are the same."* — 10,10,10,10 and 0,5,15,20 both have
  mean 10.

**Why it is an improvement.** The original raised no misconceptions explicitly. Naming the wrong
idea before it is practised is more effective than correcting it afterwards, because it forces the
student to distinguish the two.

## 4. Practice designed as three pathways, with answers that explain

**Original.** Practice lists were tagged Support/Core/Extension, but the answer section was a dense
block of numbers per section.

**This edition.** 633 questions across 11 chapters, each tagged, and 633 answers, each of which is
either a value or a short explanation. Where a question asks *why* or *explain*, the answer gives
the reasoning rather than a verdict. For example, question 7.1.9 ("A student says two angles of 50°
and 130° must be co-interior. Is that reasoning valid?") is answered: "No — many pairs add to 180°
without being co-interior; the reason must be the parallel lines."

**Why it is an improvement.** An answer key that only gives numbers teaches students to check
whether they finished, not whether they understood. The distinction is stated in the book's own
"How to use this section" note on the answers page.

## 5. Verbal reasoning made assessable

Each chapter carries two investigation cards written as Criterion B tasks — a table to build, a
pattern to describe, and a final prompt that asks the student to *justify why the rule holds for
every case, not only the cases tested*. Examples:

- Chapter 6: the growing staircase (triangular numbers, second differences) and the mosaic border.
- Chapter 7: polygon angle sums and why hexagons tile.
- Chapter 8: doubling everything (surface area versus volume) and the cheapest can.
- Chapter 10: simulating a queue, and deciding whether a game is fair.

Each Criterion D card ends with a real decision and a request for justification and an assumption,
so that criterion D's strands iii–v (justifying, predicting, evaluating) are actually exercised.

## 6. Language and reading load

- Sentences average 17 words; the longest paragraph in the book is 96 words.
- Every chapter's technical vocabulary is collected in a **vocabulary panel** at the end of the
  chapter and appears in the glossary.
- Mathematical symbols are set in italic serif with correct spacing (`3n + 2`, not `3n+2`), and
  units are spaced from their numbers as in the original's own habit pages.
- Instructions use the imperative and one action per sentence.

## 7. Framework apparatus made operational

The original listed ATL skills, criteria and inquiry questions on the planner page but rarely
referred back to them. In this edition:

- the **ATL statement** uses the required frame and names a specific skill
  (*"In order for a student to justify a generalisation, students must show that the rule
  reproduces the terms already checked and explain the structure that makes it hold for all
  terms."*);
- the **criteria table** states, for each criterion, what evidence in *this* chapter will be
  looked for;
- the **reflection page** asks one question per criterion, so the student's reflection maps onto
  the assessment.

## 9. What was deliberately *not* done

- No topic was added beyond the original's scope, and no topic was simplified to make room.
- No scaffolding was removed from the Extension pathway; Extension questions still require a
  written justification, as in the original.
- No answer was written as a bare number where the question asked for reasoning.
