KEY AND RELATED CONCEPTS
Patterns. The exponent laws are not arbitrary
rules; they record what happens when we count
repeated factors. The pattern continues
consistently from positive to zero to negative
exponents.
Quantity. Scientific notation separates the size of
a quantity (the power of 10) from its significant
digits (the mantissa), making comparisons of
scale immediate.
Representation. A number can be written in
standard form, factor form, index form or
scientific notation. Each representation
highlights a different property.

Scientific and technical innovation.

radically different scales, from the diameter
of an atom to the distance between galaxies,
and why a shared notation is essential for
collaboration.
Scientific notation is a technology of
communication as much as a mathematical
convenience. It makes the order of
magnitude visible, supports reliable
calculation with calculators and computers,
and forces the writer to state how many
digits are actually meaningful.

 Orders of magnitude and estimation
CRITERION A · III
CRITERION D · II
SCALE
An order of magnitude is a factor of 10. Comparing orders of magnitude reveals which
quantities dominate a problem and which are negligible, often before any detailed
calculation.
A useful estimation skill is to round each quantity to one significant figure and then to the nearest
power of 10. The thickness of a sheet of paper is about 
 m; the height of a room is about
 m. The ratio is about 
, so roughly thirty thousand sheets are needed to reach the
ceiling. The estimate is not exact, but it is stable: it identifies the scale of the answer.
