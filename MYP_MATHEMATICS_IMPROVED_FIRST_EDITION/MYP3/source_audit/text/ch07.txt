A photograph, a map and a shadow all keep a shape
while changing its size. Similarity explains why. It also
turns the right triangle into an instrument: one
measured angle and one measured length can reveal a
height no one can reach with a tape.

KEY AND RELATED CONCEPTS
Relationships. Similarity is a relationship
between shapes: angles stay equal while lengths
change by a common factor. Trigonometric ratios
are relationships between an angle and two sides
of a right triangle, and they hold for every
triangle with that angle.
Justification. AA, SSS and SAS similarity criteria
licence a proportion; Pythagoras' theorem and its
converse licence a right-angle claim. Each result
has conditions that must be checked before it is
used.
Measurement. Indirect measurement replaces
an inaccessible length with an accessible one
plus an angle. Precision, rounding and model
assumptions decide how much the answer can be
trusted.
GLOBAL CONTEXT AND EXPLORATION
Orientation in space and time. Exploration:
how surveyors, navigators and builders
have measured inaccessible heights and
distances, and how angle-based methods
changed maps, buildings and travel.
Before satellites, a surveyor climbed a hill
with a theodolite, read an angle and
calculated. The mathematics has not
changed: a ratio, a right triangle and a
careful record. What has changed is the
precision of the instruments and the scale
of the claims we can justify.
