An answer ends a calculation; a solution records a decision. The
entries below show the reasoning behind each result, so a difference
between your work and mine becomes a precise question rather than
a guess about what went wrong.
HOW EACH ENTRY IS ARRANGED
Exercise numbers match the chapter. Part
letters follow the number, for example 3c.
Working is shown wherever it helps you locate
a disagreement; routine arithmetic is
compressed to a single line. Units and
rounding are stated with each result.
Some questions invite more than one valid
route. The key gives one efficient method and,
where useful, a note beginning Alternatively. A
different correct method is not an error.
Validity, accuracy and a conclusion that
answers the question are what count.
Each chapter closes with a mixed practice set
and a criterion task. Mixed-practice answers
are labelled F (fluency), R (reasoning), A
(application) and E (extension) to match the
question tags. A marking guide follows the
criterion task in most chapters.
INVESTIGATIONS AND CRITERION TASKS
Open tasks receive sample responses, not a
single script: representative data, a conjecture,
a verification and a limitation. Your data may
differ. Use the sample to judge the
completeness of your table, the quality of your
generalisation and the care of your conclusion.
Academic honesty. These solutions support your
attempt; they do not replace it. If you use a sample
response, rewrite the reasoning in your own words
and explain the choice of method. That is the
evidence a teacher can assess.

generally three significant figures. Never

common source of “correct method, wrong
answer”.
