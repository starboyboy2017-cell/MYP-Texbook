KEY AND RELATED CONCEPTS
Relationships. An equation or inequality is a
condition on a variable. Solving it means finding
every value that makes the condition true, not
merely producing a number that looks plausible.
Equivalence. Each step in a solution must
preserve the solution set. Adding, subtracting,
multiplying or dividing both sides by the same
non-zero number produces an equivalent
equation; multiplying or dividing an inequality
by a negative number calls for special care.
Systems. Two conditions at once restrict the
possible values more than either condition alone.
Geometrically, a system of two linear equations
asks where two lines meet; a system of
inequalities describes a region of possible
allocations.
GLOBAL CONTEXT AND EXPLORATION
Globalization and sustainability.
Exploration: how communities allocate
limited land, water, energy and labour
among competing uses, and how
mathematical constraints make the trade-
offs visible.
Sustainability is not only an environmental
idea; it is a problem of allocation. A garden,
a clinic or a workshop has finite resources.
Writing the limits as inequalities turns a
debate about priorities into a set of feasible
plans that can be compared, tested and
questioned.

substitution
CRITERION A · II
CRITERION C · III
SYSTEMS
A single equation with two variables has infinitely many solutions. Two equations
together can pin down a single pair — if the conditions are independent and consistent.
The graphical method makes the meaning visible: plot both lines and read the intersection. It is
approximate unless the intersection lies exactly on grid lines. The substitution method is exact:
solve one equation for one variable, substitute that expression into the other equation, and solve
the resulting one-variable equation.

Chapter summary and review
KEY IDEAS
Equivalent equations have the same solution
set; each operation must be applied to both
sides.
Clearing brackets and fractions before
isolating the variable reduces errors.
An identity has infinitely many solutions; a
contradiction has none.
Multiplying or dividing an inequality by a
negative number reverses its direction.
A solution set can be shown on a number
line or written in interval notation.
Simultaneous equations can be solved
graphically, by substitution or by
elimination.
Parallel lines give no solution; coincident
lines give infinitely many.
A system of inequalities has a feasible
region; a linear objective is optimised at a
vertex, subject to integer constraints.
